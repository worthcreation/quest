Quest is a cozy, hand-made browser RPG in the spirit of Zelda and EarthBound, built for a family to play on a laptop or phone. You wake on an old jetty beside Pip, a small, earnest friend who shows you the ropes: coax seeds from a robin, plant a garden, gather stones, sticks and rabbit fluff, and set up camp. Then gremlins carry Pip off into the woods at dusk, and you find a sword, a cave and a way home on drifting spores.

The mood is warm, curious and a little silly (rabbit glue, taunting gremlins), never mean. Puzzles are physical and readable: see the thing, understand what it wants, do it with your hands. Skills grow quietly with use, Pip teaches by doing, and the world explains itself instead of through menus.

Where it's headed: the mountain as one family of screens out of the rise (each built fresh, some fixed and zoomed out, some moving; jumps you can read; rabbits that grow buffer and crankier with height, hawks that hunt them, wind that carries dust and grit higher up), with secrets on every screen and side quests along the way: an ornithologist after rare eggs who wants to try carrot juice, a tortoise in its sheltered hollow trading peach stones for peaches, trees to grow and harvest, a windmill by a field mushroom. Then the High Reaches above the clouds, crafting on the fly in two or three presses with tools that wear and grow sturdier as you do, a sword you earn by going downriver to meet Old Wick and bringing fire to its brambles, mushrooms as places to rest and revive, a maze of light in the Hollow, then a second chapter under the swamp shrine.
